## How this evaluation works

- **Fresh samples, never trained on.** Generator A as v1 knew it (`synthetic.ts` version 1, the Phase A baseline sample) and generator A now (version 2): 20000 people each, seed 7001. Generator B (`synthetic-b.ts`, version 1): 20000 people, seed 7002.
- **Generator B** was designed separately with different assumptions (latent-factor tastes instead of bundles, bimodal engagement, linear adoption, action counts tied strongly to app use and overdispersed, flatter labels with a status-quo bias, 8.0% label noise, current schema only). It is never used for training, thresholds, calibration or tuning, and was frozen before v2 was designed. The same person wrote both generators, so B tests specific assumptions; it is not independent evidence.
- **People the app can score.** `PersonalisationProvider` only scores people who consented, passes their settings through the cookie's `sanitize()` (current schema), and never passes Make it yours ticks (`choices: null`). Every metric below uses exactly that population unless it says "as reported".
- **Coverage, repetition and cross-mode** run the real `recommendPreferences()`, `recordOutcome()`, `reconcile()`, `onPrefsEvent()` and `forVisit()`, one suggestion at a time like the provider.

## Retraining rule (fixed before v2 was built)

A new model replaces `model.json` only if, on these fresh samples, all of these hold:

1. **Test AUC.** On generator A as v1 knew it, and on the new generator A: mean AUC at least v1's, and no target more than 0.01 below v1.
2. **Calibration.** Mean ECE (after any calibration step) below v1's on both generator A samples.
3. **Robustness.** On generator B: mean AUC above v1's, and the pooled keep rate of everything offered no lower than v1's.
4. **Keep rate.** Every target the new model offers is kept by at least 80% of the people offered it, on both generator A samples.

### What the baseline shows

- **Served vs reported.** `largerText` reaches 0.14% of eligible people (EVALUATION.md: 1.5%); `calmLayout` reaches 5.88% of eligible people (EVALUATION.md: 7.0%); `voiceFirst` reaches 0.68% of eligible people (EVALUATION.md: 1.2%). The gap is the population: the app only scores people who consented, on the current schema, without Make it yours ticks.
- **Calibration.** Mean ECE 0.035 on generator A and 0.065 on generator B. No prediction on A reaches 0.9: the forest squeezes probabilities towards the base rate, so thresholds sit in a narrow band.
- **Robustness.** Mean AUC falls from 0.680 (A) to 0.559 (B); the same pipeline trained on B itself only reaches 0.563, so B is hard to learn from these inputs at all. The bigger problem is the keep rate of what is offered: 84.3% on A but 56.5% on B. The 80% promise holds only under generator A's assumptions.
- **Coverage.** 6.0% of people the app can score ever see a suggestion; 87% of what is shown is `calmLayout`. 5 of 8 settings are never offered.
- **Repetition.** 80% of people who always say No thanks see the same suggestion again after they change any setting (it may come back once per change until the second no). People who always say Not now see 2.4 suggestions over 6 visits. Most in one visit: 3.
- **Cross-mode.** Any suggestion: 9.4% in Simple view vs 0.4% in Detailed view (generator A). `voiceFirst` disappears entirely when the same people switch to Detailed. With talking first on: 3.8%.
